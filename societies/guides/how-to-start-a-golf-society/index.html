How to start a golf society: a step-by-step guide

To start a golf society, get three or four golfers into a WhatsApp group, pick a name, agree how often you’ll play, how handicaps work and what it costs, then book your first golf day at a club’s society rate. Aim for 16 to 24 members, collect money before each day, and keep fixtures, sign-ups and payments in one free link.

How to start a golf society in 8 steps

  1. Get your first four-ball together. Every golf society starts with three or four golfers who fancy playing together more often. Mates from work, the pub, the gym or the school gate are the usual beginnings. Start a WhatsApp group with them: that’s your society’s home from day one.
  2. Pick a name. Something your members will happily say out loud in a clubhouse. Ideas are further down this page.
  3. Decide how often you’ll play. Monthly from April to September suits most golf societies, with a trip or a winter day if there’s appetite. Fix the day of the week early (Saturday mornings and Friday afternoons are the usual favourites) so members can plan around it.
  4. Agree how handicaps work. Members with an official handicap index use it. For everyone else, a society handicap from a few scores does the job. In England, golfers who aren’t club members can get an official handicap through England Golf’s iGolf scheme. Our guide to golf society handicap systems covers the options.
  5. Write five simple rules. Fees, paying before the day, drop-outs, handicaps and ties. Copy our golf society rules template into the group and change what you like.
  6. Set the money side. Members pay the green fee and food at cost, plus a small society entry (often £5 to £15) for prizes and the trophy fund. The society day cost guide has a calculator. Collect before the day: “not paid, not playing” saves every argument.
  7. Book your first golf day. Ring or email two or three clubs and ask for their society package. Most want a minimum number (we’ve seen 8, 12 and 16 in published 2026 society rates), a deposit and final numbers a week or two before. Spring and summer dates go first, so book months ahead.
  8. Put it all in one link. Start your society in Society HQ (free, about two minutes) and send the link to the group. Members tap in, out or maybe, the waiting list sorts itself, you tick who’s paid, and every golf day gets its own Form Guide preview and report.

How big should a golf society be?

A golf society can be a single four-ball. But most golf clubs’ society rates need a minimum group, commonly between 8 and 16 golfers, and some golfers always drop out. So aim for 16 to 24 members to fill 12 to 16 places on a typical society day. Bigger is fine: Society HQ’s waiting list moves the next golfer up automatically when someone drops out.

Mistakes new golf society organisers make

  1. Paying the club before the members pay you. You end up chasing money for months. Collect first.
  2. Booking too late. The best spring and summer dates at good-value clubs go months ahead.
  3. No rule for drop-outs. Decide up front whether late drop-outs pay. Most societies say yes after final numbers have gone to the club.
  4. Doing it all yourself. Pick someone to help: results, the trophy, the WhatsApp banter. In Society HQ, make them co-organiser.
  5. Making it too serious. Keep the rules short and the prizes fun. The awards night is half the point.

Once you’re up and running

How to start a golf society (UK): questions organisers ask

How do I start a golf society?

Get three or four golfers together in a WhatsApp group, pick a name, agree how often you’ll play, how handicaps work and what it costs, then book your first golf day at a club’s society rate. A free organiser like Society HQ keeps fixtures, sign-ups and who’s paid in one link.

How many people do you need to start a golf society?

You can start with four. To get golf clubs’ society rates, which commonly need between 8 and 16 golfers on the day, aim for 16 to 24 members.

Do you need to be a golf club member to start a golf society?

No. Golf societies are groups of golfers who play together at different courses, and most members of most societies don’t belong to a club. Clubs welcome societies as visitors at society rates.

Do golf society members need an official handicap?

Not usually. Members with an official handicap index use it, and the society gives everyone else a society handicap. In England, golfers without a club can get an official handicap through England Golf’s iGolf scheme.

How much does it cost to start a golf society?

Nothing. Setting up the WhatsApp group and Society HQ is free. Members pay each golf day’s green fee and food at cost, plus a small society entry for prizes.

Does a golf society need insurance or a bank account?

Neither is required to start. Ask each golf club what it needs from a visiting society, and decide as a group whether you want a separate account for the society’s money once it grows.
