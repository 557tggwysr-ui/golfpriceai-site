What do I need to shoot?

How we work it out

Your rounds are compared with the average golfer at the level you’re chasing. The averages come from Shot Scope’s shot-tracking data, published by Golf Monthly. Scores are on a par 72, and we adjust for your course’s par.

  • Putting: your putts minus theirs.
  • Penalties: every penalty shot counts in full. There’s no reliable published average by handicap, so we don’t guess one.
  • Approach shots: each green you miss beyond theirs costs a shot unless you get up and down, at the rate they do.
  • Short game and the rest: whatever’s left of the gap in score. Chips, bunkers, the odd duff, and any number you skipped.

Shot Saver: questions

Where do most amateur golfers lose shots?

For most club golfers it isn't the driver. Shot Scope's data shows a 20-handicapper hits almost as many fairways as a scratch golfer (45% against 50%), but far fewer greens (14% against 59%) and takes three or four more putts. Penalties, approach shots and putting are where most shots go.

How does Shot Saver work out where my shots go?

You add five numbers from your scorecard. We compare them with the published Shot Scope averages for the level you're chasing. Putts and penalty shots count one for one. Greens you miss beyond theirs cost a shot unless you get up and down, at the rate they do. Whatever's left of the gap in score goes to short game and the rest. We average your last five rounds.

Do I need a shot tracker or an app?

No. Everything comes off a normal scorecard, as long as you jot down fairways, greens, putts and penalties as you go. Skip any number you didn't keep.

Is my data saved anywhere?

Your rounds are kept in this browser and shown in your My Clubhouse. Nothing personal is sent to us: we only count, anonymously, how many rounds get added.

Why do penalty shots count in full?

We couldn't find a reliable published average for penalty shots by handicap, so rather than guess one, every penalty shot counts. Good golfers have fewer, but none of them are compulsory.
